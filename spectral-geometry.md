# Spectral geometry of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\): the state of the art

This document surveys what is currently known about the spectral geometry of the Picard group \(\Gamma = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) and its orbifold \(M = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3\), as groundwork for connecting it to the Schmidt-arrangement project (see [outlook.md](outlook.md) §3.1 and the companion [spectral-outlook.md](spectral-outlook.md)). It is a working reference, not a textbook: each section states the sharpest results we could verify, with sources, and closes with what is *missing* — the gaps are collected in §14 and become the program of the companion outlook.

**Verification status.** Every citation was checked against a fetched source (original PDFs where possible) during the literature pass of 2026-08-28/29, except items marked **[std]** (standard references quoted for context) or **[UNVERIFIED]**. Three facts most relevant to us were *confirmed absent* from the literature (§14).

**Conventions.** \(\mathbb{H}^3 = \{(z, r) : z \in \mathbb{C},\ r > 0\}\) with metric \((|dz|^2 + dr^2)/r^2\), volume element \(r^{-3}\,dz\,dr\); \(\Delta\) the (negative) Laplace–Beltrami operator \(r^2(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + \partial_r^2) - r\,\partial_r\). Eigenvalues are written
$$
\lambda = s(2-s) = 1 + t^2, \qquad s = 1 + it,
$$
so the tempered range is \(\lambda \ge 1\) (\(t \in \mathbb{R}\)), and \(\lambda_0 = 0\) (\(s = 2\)) belongs to the constants. \(K = \mathbb{Q}(i)\), \(\zeta_K(s) = \zeta(s)L(s, \chi_{-4})\), and \(G = L(2,\chi_{-4})\) is Catalan's constant.

## 1. The Picard orbifold

\(M = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\backslash\mathbb{H}^3\) is a non-compact, finite-volume hyperbolic 3-orbifold with one cusp (class number one). Humbert's volume formula \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)\backslash\mathbb{H}^3) = \tfrac{1}{4\pi^2}|d_K|^{3/2}\zeta_K(2)\) (as used e.g. in Parkkonen–Paulin 2011) gives for \(K = \mathbb{Q}(i)\):
$$
\boxed{\ \operatorname{vol}(M) \;=\; \frac{8\,\zeta_K(2)}{4\pi^2} \;=\; \frac{2}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6}\,G \;=\; \frac{G}{3} \;=\; 0.30532186\ldots\ }
$$
The Catalan constant of [euclidean-counting.md](euclidean-counting.md) *is* the volume of the Bianchi orbifold: the Euclidean counting theorem \(\sum_{n \le X} N_e(n) = X^2/2G + O(X\log X)\) reads
$$
\sum_{n\le X} N_e(n) \;=\; \frac{X^2}{6\operatorname{vol}(M)} + O(X\log X),
$$
which is the shape that every equidistribution-theoretic circle count predicts (§13). This is the first entry of the dictionary between the project and spectral geometry.

**Symmetries.** \(M\) carries the orientation-reversing isometry \(z \mapsto \bar z\) (Galois conjugation, the \(\theta\) of [involution.md](involution.md)) and the isometry induced by \(\operatorname{diag}(i,1)\)-conjugation (which swaps \(\mathcal{S}\) and \(i\mathcal{S}\)); together with \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) they generate the extended Picard group. The Laplace spectrum accordingly splits into four symmetry classes (§3).

**Totally geodesic surfaces.** The geodesic planes of \(\mathbb{H}^3\) are hemispheres over circles/lines in \(\hat{\mathbb{C}}\); a plane descends to an immersed totally geodesic 2-suborbifold of \(M\) iff its boundary circle is \(K\)-rational, i.e. given by an integral binary Hermitian form \(A|z|^2 + \bar B z + B\bar z + C = 0\) of discriminant \(D = |B|^2 - AC > 0\). The stabilizer of such a circle in \(\Gamma\) is (the unit group of an order in) the quaternion algebra \(\bigl(\tfrac{-1,\,D}{\mathbb{Q}}\bigr)\), an arithmetic Fuchsian group; it is **cocompact iff the form is anisotropic over \(K\) iff the circle contains no point of \(\mathbb{P}^1(K)\)** (Maclachlan 1986; Maclachlan–Reid 1991; James–Maclachlan 1996; Maclachlan–Reid, *The Arithmetic of Hyperbolic 3-Manifolds*, Ch. 9).
- \(\hat{\mathbb{R}}\) (discriminant \(1\), isotropic): stabilizer a copy of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) (Stange 2018a, Prop. 3.4) — the **modular surface** \(Y \hookrightarrow M\), non-compact, area \(\pi/3\) as a \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\)-quotient. Its \(\Gamma\)-orbit of boundary circles is the Schmidt arrangement \(\mathcal{S}\).
- \(|z|^2 = 2\) is *isotropic* (\(1+i\) lies on it): a *cusped* geodesic surface, not compact. The smallest **closed** geodesic surface comes from \(|z|^2 = 3\) (anisotropic since \(3\) is not a norm from \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\)).

**Abundance of surfaces and arithmeticity.** \(M\), being arithmetic with one geodesic surface, contains infinitely many incommensurable maximal ones (commensurator argument; Maclachlan–Reid Ch. 9). Conversely Bader–Fisher–Miller–Stover (Ann. of Math. 193 (2021) 837–861): *a finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold containing infinitely many maximal totally geodesic surfaces is arithmetic* (independently Margulis–Mohammadi (ETDS 42 (2022)) for closed manifolds). The correct biconditional: infinitely many \(\iff\) arithmetic *and at least one*. So **the very existence of the Schmidt arrangement — infinitely many circles — is a certificate of arithmeticity**; a non-arithmetic 3-manifold has at most finitely many such circles, and generically none. In finite volume every geodesic plane is closed or dense (Shah 1991; Ratner 1991). Counting the surfaces by area: linear growth \(\Pi(x) \sim cx\), proved for \(d \equiv 3 \bmod 4\) Bianchi orbifolds by Jung (J. Number Theory 202 (2019); the Gaussian case is explicitly excluded there) and for \(d = 2\) with an explicit constant by A. Lee (arXiv:2601.15700).

## 2. Spectral decomposition and Eisenstein series

For the one-cusped \(M\) (Elstrodt–Grunewald–Mennicke, *Groups Acting on Hyperbolic Space*, Springer 1998 — henceforth **EGM98** — Chs. 3–6, 8; earlier Tanigawa 1977, Szmidt 1983, EGM Crelle 360 (1985) 160–213):
$$
L^2(M) \;=\; \mathbb{C}\cdot 1 \ \oplus\ L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}(M)\ \oplus\ \operatorname{Eis},
$$
with \(\operatorname{Eis}\) spanned by the analytic continuation of the Eisenstein series
$$
E(P, s) \;=\; \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma} r(\gamma P)^{1+s}, \qquad \operatorname{Re} s > 1,
$$
whose constant term is \(r^{1+s} + \varphi(s)\,r^{1-s}\). The scattering "matrix" is scalar and is a ratio of completed Dedekind zeta functions of \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\),
$$
\varphi(s) \;=\; \frac{\pi}{s}\,\frac{\zeta_K(s)}{\zeta_K(s+1)}
$$
in the normalization of EGM98 Ch. 8 §4 **[std** — shape verified: the residue at \(s=1\) equals \(\pi \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)/\zeta_K(2) = 3/(2G) = 1/(2\operatorname{vol} M)\), matching the general residue formula\(]\). Consequences: the continuous spectrum is \([1,\infty)\) with multiplicity one; the resonances/poles of \(E(P,s)\) in \(\operatorname{Re} s < 1\) are governed by the **zeros of \(\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}\)** — the Riemann hypothesis for \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) is a statement about the resonances of the Picard orbifold.

**Small eigenvalues.** \(M\) has *no* eigenvalues in \((0,1)\): the smallest positive eigenvalue is \(\lambda_1 \approx 44.85\) (§3), far above the continuous-spectrum threshold \(1\). So the exceptional-eigenvalue terms that appear in the general cofinite theory (e.g. in Sarnak's prime geodesic theorem, §6) are vacuous for the Picard group.

## 3. The cuspidal spectrum: Maass forms on \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\)

**Weyl law** (EGM98, Theorem 8.9.1): the cuspidal counting function obeys
$$
N_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\lambda) \;\sim\; \frac{\operatorname{vol}(M)}{6\pi^2}\,\lambda^{3/2} \;=\; \frac{G}{18\pi^2}\,\lambda^{3/2},
$$
so cusp forms exist in abundance (no Phillips–Sarnak destruction issue at level 1 — the arithmetic symmetry protects them). Sanity check against numerics: the prediction gives \(\approx 14{,}150\) eigenvalues below \(\lambda = 19601\), versus \(13{,}950\) computed (below) — the deficit is the negative Eisenstein/scattering correction term.

**Numerics.** The first systematic computation is Steil, *Eigenvalues of the Laplacian for Bianchi groups* (in: Emerging Applications of Number Theory, IMA Vol. 109, Springer 1999), using Hecke operators and coefficient relations. The quantum-chaos/cosmology school (Then; Aurich–Steiner–Then, arXiv:gr-qc/0404020; arXiv:0712.4322) extended this to **13,950 consecutive eigenvalues** in \(1 < \lambda = 1 + t^2 \le 19601\) (\(t \le 140\)), falling into the four symmetry classes \(D, G, C, H\) with counts \(4115, 2805, 3715, 3315\). The smallest cuspidal eigenvalue of the Picard orbifold is
$$
t_1 = 6.6221193402528\ldots, \qquad \lambda_1 = 1 + t_1^2 \approx 44.85.
$$
There is no LMFDB database of *Maass* Bianchi forms yet (the LMFDB "Bianchi modular forms" are the weight-2 cohomological forms of Cremona et al. **[std]**); rigorous certification of Bianchi Maass eigenvalues in the style of Booker–Strömbergsson–Venkatesh appears not to exist **[UNVERIFIED — worth a fresh search before quoting in print]**.

**Hecke theory and the arithmetic classification.** \(L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}\) carries Hecke operators \(T_\mathfrak{n}\) (\(\mathfrak{n} \subset \mathbb{Z}[i]\)); the joint eigenfunctions are Hecke–Maass forms, corresponding to cuspidal automorphic representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_K)\) unramified everywhere with trivial central character. Three known sources:
1. **Base change** from \(\mathbb{Q}\) (Langlands, *Base Change for* \(\mathrm{GL}(2)\), Ann. of Math. Studies 96, 1980 **[std]**): each level-1 (or appropriate level-4, nebentypus \(\chi_{-4}\)) Maass form on \(\mathbb{Q}\) lifts; the lift of a form with eigenvalue \(\tfrac14 + r^2\) has \(t = 2r\). Base-change forms are characterized by \(\pi^\sigma \cong \pi\) and — crucially for us — by *period/distinction* criteria (§9).
2. **CM (dihedral) forms** from Grossencharacters of quadratic extensions of \(K\) **[std]**.
3. **Genuinely new forms** — everything else; density considerations (Weyl law vs. the 2-dimensional count of the base-change source) show base-change and CM forms have density zero among all Bianchi Maass forms.

The *cohomological* spectrum (weight-2 Bianchi modular forms, i.e. specific non-tempered K-types with cuspidal cohomology \(H^1_{\mathrm{cusp}}\)) is sparse for \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\): the level-1 cuspidal cohomology vanishes, and its growth in congruence towers is governed by limit-multiplicity and torsion phenomena (§10; Finis–Grunewald–Tirao, Exp. Math. 19 (2010) **[std]**; Şengün, Exp. Math. 20 (2011) 487–505).

## 4. The Ramanujan–Selberg problem over \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\)

The analogue of Selberg's \(\tfrac14\)-conjecture: **congruence Bianchi orbifolds should have no eigenvalues below 1** (equivalently, cuspidal spectral parameters \(t \in \mathbb{R}\)). Known:

- Blomer–Brumley, *On the Ramanujan conjecture over number fields* (Ann. of Math. 174 (2011) 581–605, arXiv:1003.0559): the Kim–Sarnak exponent \(\theta = 7/64\) holds for \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) over *every* number field, at *all* places including complex archimedean ones. At a complex place the unramified parameter \(|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{\pm\sigma}\) with \(|\operatorname{Re}\sigma| \le 7/64\) gives Laplace eigenvalue \(\lambda = 1 - (2\sigma)^2\) (the module \(|\cdot|_\mathbb{C} = |\cdot|^2\) doubles the exponent; check: trivial representation \(\sigma = \tfrac12 \mapsto \lambda = 0\)), hence for every congruence subgroup of a Bianchi group
$$
\boxed{\ \lambda_1 \;\ge\; 1 - \left(\tfrac{7}{32}\right)^2 \;=\; \tfrac{975}{1024} \;=\; 0.95215\ldots\ }
$$
- Earlier bounds over number fields: Gelbart–Jacquet (\(\lambda_1 \ge 1 - (\tfrac12)^2\cdot\) — i.e. any \(\theta < \tfrac14\)), Luo–Rudnick–Sarnak (\(\theta \le \tfrac15\) over number fields), Kim–Shahidi (\(\theta \le \tfrac19\)) **[std]**.
- For \(\Gamma = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) itself the question is vacuously settled far beyond the conjecture by the numerics (\(\lambda_1 \approx 44.85\), §3); no *proof* that \(\lambda_1 \ge 1\) at level 1 seems to be in print beyond the general \(975/1024\) **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## 5. Trace formulas: Selberg, Selberg zeta, Kuznetsov

**Selberg trace formula.** For cofinite Kleinian groups, established in this setting by Tanigawa (1977), Szmidt (1983), and EGM (Crelle 360 (1985); EGM98 Ch. 5–6). For a point-pair invariant with transform \(h\):
$$
\sum_j h(t_j) \;+\; (\text{Eis: } \varphi'/\varphi\text{-integral}) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{vol}(M)}{(2\pi)^{?}}\,(\text{identity term}) + \sum_{\text{elliptic}} + \sum_{\text{loxodromic}} + (\text{parabolic}),
$$
where the loxodromic sum runs over conjugacy classes of loxodromic \(\gamma\), each contributing through its **complex length** \(\ell(\gamma) + i\theta(\gamma)\) (\(\operatorname{tr}\gamma = 2\cosh\tfrac{\ell + i\theta}{2}\)) and the norm \(N(\gamma) = e^{\ell(\gamma)}\). (Exact term shapes: EGM98 Ch. 6 **[std]**.)

**The length spectrum is class-number arithmetic over \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\).** Loxodromic conjugacy classes of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) correspond to equivalence classes of binary quadratic forms over \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\); Sarnak's *The arithmetic and geometry of some hyperbolic three manifolds* (Acta Math. 151 (1983) 253–295) exploits this to evaluate averages of class numbers of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)-forms via the trace formula — the 3-dimensional sibling of the classical "class numbers of real quadratic forms via closed geodesics". The project's Cartan bridge ([involution.md](involution.md) §2) produces the special loxodromic classes with *real* trace \(-2n\), i.e. \(\theta = 0\), \(\ell = 2\operatorname{arccosh} n\); these sit inside this dictionary as the "totally real" stratum (see [spectral-outlook.md](spectral-outlook.md) §5).

**Selberg zeta function.** \(Z_\Gamma(s) = \prod_{\gamma_0}\prod_{k_1,k_2 \ge 0}(1 - a(\gamma_0)^{-2k_1}\bar a(\gamma_0)^{-2k_2}N(\gamma_0)^{-s})\) over primitive loxodromic classes converges for \(\operatorname{Re} s > 2\), continues meromorphically, and has zeros at the spectral parameters (EGM98 **[std]**; Nakasuji (2000/2001) proved the *spectral explicit formula* for it and re-derived the prime geodesic theorem below). No dynamical (transfer-operator) expression of \(Z_\Gamma\) exists for any Bianchi group — see §12.

**Kuznetsov/sum formula.** Motohashi (1996–97) established an explicit spherical Kuznetsov formula for \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\); Bruggeman–Motohashi, *Sum formula for Kloosterman sums and fourth moment of the Dedekind zeta-function over the Gaussian number field* (Funct. Approx. Comment. Math. 31 (2003) 23–92) proved the full sum formula for \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\backslash\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})\) and derived an explicit spectral expansion of \(\int |\zeta_K(\tfrac12 + it)|^4\) — the Gaussian-field analogue of the Motohashi fourth-moment formula. A thesis of Lokvenec-Guleska extends the sum formula to general imaginary quadratic fields **[UNVERIFIED]**. Newest: Zhi Qi, *On the symmetric square large sieve for \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\backslash\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})\)…* (arXiv:2407.17959, Sci. China Math.): a spectral large sieve for symmetric squares, improving the second moment of \(L(\tfrac12, \operatorname{sym}^2 u_j)\) from \(T^{3+2/3+\varepsilon}\) (Balkanova–Frolenkov) to \(T^{3+1/2+\varepsilon}\).

## 6. The prime geodesic theorem for the Picard orbifold

Let \(\Psi_\Gamma(x) = \sum_{N(P) \le x}\Lambda_\Gamma(P)\) over loxodromic classes (\(\Lambda_\Gamma(P) = \log N(P_0)\)). Since \(M\) has no small eigenvalues, the main term is \(x^2/2\), and \(\mathcal{E}_\Gamma(x) = \Psi_\Gamma(x) - x^2/2\). The verified chronology (from Kaneko, *The prime geodesic theorem for the Picard orbifold*, arXiv:2403.06626v2, Table 1 — read directly):

| 1983 | Sarnak, Acta 151, Thm 5.1 | \(5/3\) | any cofinite Kleinian group |
| 2019 | Balkanova–Chatzakos–Cherubini–Frolenkov–Laaksonen | \(13/8\) | Luo–Sarnak method over \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) |
| 2022 | Balog–Biró–Cherubini–Laaksonen, Cor. 1.4 | \(67/42\) | \(= \tfrac32 + \tfrac{4\vartheta}{7}\), \(\vartheta = \tfrac16\) |
| 2022 | Balkanova–Frolenkov, Thm 1.1 | \(376/237\) | |
| 2024 | Zhi Qi, Thm 3 | \(242/153\) | via the symmetric-square large sieve |
| 2025 | **Kaneko, Thm 1.3** | \(\mathbf{255/172} = 1.48256\ldots\) | **current unconditional record; first to break \(3/2\)** |
| 2001\(^\dagger\) | Koyama | \(11/7\) | \(\dagger\): under mean Lindelöf |
| 2020\(^\dagger\) | Balkanova–Frolenkov, Thm 1.2 | \(3/2\) | under GLH |

Here \(\vartheta\) is the subconvexity exponent for quadratic Dirichlet \(L\)-functions over \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) in the conductor aspect (current record \(\vartheta = 1/6\), Nelson); Kaneko's Theorem 1.1 gives \(\mathcal{E}_\Gamma(x) \ll x^{(23+6\vartheta)/16+\varepsilon} + x^{(7+3\vartheta)/5+\varepsilon}\). The barrier \(x^{3/2}\), long considered intrinsic "owing to the abundance of eigenvalues in Weyl's law (EGM98, Thm 8.9.1)", was first surpassed unconditionally by Kaneko's Theorem 1.3. Second moments: Chatzakos–Cherubini–Laaksonen, Math. Z. 300 (2022) 791–806, and Kaneko (ANT 16 (2022) 1845, spectral exponential sums) — mean-square results of strength \(x^{3/2+\varepsilon}\)-type in short intervals. Expected optimal exponent: \(1 + \varepsilon\).

For the project: the closed geodesics of [involution.md](involution.md) have \(N(\gamma) = \varepsilon_n^2 = (n + \sqrt{n^2-1})^2\), so they are counted by \(\Psi_\Gamma\) with \(x \asymp 4n^2\) — the PGT machinery (Kuznetsov over \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\), Zagier-type \(L\)-series, spectral exponential sums) is exactly the toolbox available for refined counts along the Schmidt stratum.

## 7. Sup-norms and the geodesic-surface anomaly

For a compact Riemannian 3-manifold the local bound is \(\|\phi\|_\infty \ll \lambda^{1/2}\). On *arithmetic* hyperbolic 3-manifolds the truth is different in a way that directly involves the geodesic surfaces (all verified from Blomer–Harcos–Milićević, Duke Math. J. 165 (2016) 625–659, arXiv:1401.5154, whose setting is exactly \(\Gamma_0(N) \subset \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\)):

- **Lower bound (the anomaly).** Rudnick–Sarnak (compact case) and Milićević: on any arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold of **Maclachlan–Reid type** — i.e. one *containing immersed totally geodesic surfaces* — there is an infinite orthonormal family of cusp forms with
$$
\|\phi\|_\infty \;\gg\; \lambda^{1/4 - \varepsilon}.
$$
This falsifies the naive random-wave/subconvexity expectation \(\|\phi\|_\infty \ll_\varepsilon \lambda^\varepsilon\)-ish for 3-manifolds. BHM: "the coincidence between the discrete layers of power growth and the immersed geometric features is extremely intriguing"; the mechanism is a dense family of points with large rational stabilizers — the same algebraic structure (invariant trace field \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), fixed quaternion algebra) that produces the geodesic surfaces. **The Schmidt arrangement is the boundary picture of exactly this feature of the Picard orbifold**: the sup-norm anomaly of arithmetic 3-manifolds is, geometrically, the abundance of Schmidt circles.
- **Upper bounds** (BHM Thms 1–3, for Hecke–Maass newforms on \(\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}^3\), \(N \in \mathbb{Z}[i]\) squarefree, \(T = 1 + \sqrt\lambda\), \(V \asymp |N|^{2+o(1)}\)): \(\|\phi\|_\infty \ll T V^{-1/6}(TV)^\varepsilon\) (volume aspect), \(\|\phi\|_\infty \ll T^{5/6}(TV)^\varepsilon\) (eigenvalue aspect; i.e. \(\lambda^{5/12+\varepsilon}\)), and the hybrid \(\|\phi\|_\infty \ll (TV^{1/2})^{-1/9}\,T\,(TV)^\varepsilon\). (An earlier claimed eigenvalue-aspect saving by Koyama has a gap — BHM footnote 2.)

## 8. Quantum chaos on the Picard orbifold

- **Eisenstein QE/QUE.** Koyama, *Quantum ergodicity of Eisenstein series for arithmetic 3-manifolds* (Comm. Math. Phys. 215 (2000) 477–486): the Luo–Sarnak theorem transposed to \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)\), class number one, worked out for the Picard group — microlocal lifts of \(E(\cdot, 1+it)\) equidistribute as \(t \to \infty\). Kim–Lee (arXiv:2603.16518, 2026) extend to all Bianchi groups (any class number).
- **AQUE for cusp forms.** For Hecke–Maass forms on arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds, quantum unique ergodicity appears to be **open** — Lindenstrauss's measure-rigidity proof is for surfaces (and Silberman–Venkatesh for higher rank); we found no published extension to \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})\) **[UNVERIFIED as a sweep — re-check before quoting]**. Numerical QUE evidence exists in the Aurich–Steiner–Then computations.
- **Restriction problems.** Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov give the local bound \(\lambda^{1/4}\) for restrictions to surfaces **[std]**; Jiaqi Hou (arXiv:2304.04863, Math. Z., and arXiv:2410.17164) proves power savings for periods and restrictions of Hecke–Maass forms over totally geodesic surfaces in *compact* arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds, via amplification: \(P_Y(\psi) \ll \lambda^{-1/74+\varepsilon}\) for the period, plus \(L^2\)-restriction and geodesic-tube bounds. The non-compact (Bianchi/modular-surface) case is untouched. QUE *along* a geodesic surface (equidistribution of \(|\phi|^2\big|_Y\)) is open in 3D.

## 9. Periods, distinction, and the relative toolbox for \((\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C}), \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R}))\)

This is the automorphic framework for integrals over the modular surface \(Y \subset M\) — the spectral side of any Schmidt-arrangement trace formula. All items verified from the original papers.

- **Distinction \(\iff\) Asai pole \(\iff\) base change.** For cuspidal \(\pi\) on \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_E)\), \(E/F\) quadratic, with central character trivial on \(\mathbb{A}_F^\times\): \(\pi\) is *distinguished* (some vector has nonvanishing period over \(\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\)) iff the Asai (twisted tensor) \(L\)-function \(L(s, \pi, \mathrm{As})\) has a (simple) pole at \(s = 1\), iff \(\pi\) is the base change of a cuspidal \(\pi_0/F\) with central character \(\omega_{E/F}\). Flicker (Bull. SMF 116 (1988) 295–313) under a split-archimedean hypothesis; **Flicker–Zinoviev (Proc. Japan Acad. 71A (1995) 114–116) removed it — so the criterion holds for \(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}\)**, where \(\omega_{E/F} = \chi_{-4}\). Local theory: Flicker (Crelle 418 (1991)); Kable (Amer. J. Math. 126 (2004)); Anandavardhanan–Kable–Tandon (Proc. AMS 132 (2004)); Matringe (IMRN 2011). The factorization \(L(s, \pi\times\pi^\sigma) = L(s,\mathrm{As}\,\pi)\,L(s, \mathrm{As}\,\pi\otimes\omega_{E/F})\).
- **The \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) caveats (our actual pair).** Anandavardhanan–Prasad (Math. Res. Lett. 10 (2003); Amer. J. Math. 128 (2006); Compositio 149 (2013)): for \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), distinction is packet-sensitive, local multiplicities can exceed one, the global period is **not** always a product of local functionals, and there are cuspidal \(\pi\) with identically vanishing period although every local constituent is distinguished. Any period formula for \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) must respect this.
- **Relative trace formula.** Jacquet–Lai (Compositio 54 (1985) 243–310): the founding RTF for the Galois pair; geometric side = double cosets \(H\gamma H\) parametrized via \(g \mapsto g\bar g^{-1}\) (Hilbert 90 / Cartan map — *literally the involution machinery of [involution.md](involution.md), adelically*); the residue mechanism \(\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\int\varphi\, E(\cdot,s) = \) period. Ye (Crelle 400 (1989)) computed the relative (Kloosterman) orbital integrals for base change. Spectral side: Lapid–Rogawski, *Periods of Eisenstein series: the Galois case* (Duke 120 (2003)) — intertwining periods; Chaudouard (Selecta Math. 2025, arXiv:2204.06069) — an absolutely convergent full spectral expansion for \(\mathrm{GL}_n(E)/\mathrm{GL}_n(F)\).
- **Toric periods** (for the closed geodesics): Waldspurger; explicit over any number field by Martin–Whitehouse (IMRN 2009), whose §5 already applies the formula to equidistribution of closed geodesics on hyperbolic 3-folds.
- **What does not exist:** a closed-form evaluation of the period of a Picard Eisenstein series or Bianchi Maass form over the immersed modular surface, with its arithmetic (class-number) expansion. Confirmed absent (§14).

## 10. Spectral invariants beyond eigenvalues: torsion, isospectrality

- **Torsion homology growth** (analytic torsion = spectral). Bergeron–Venkatesh (J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 12 (2013) 391–447, read directly): for cocompact congruence towers of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})\)-lattices and strongly acyclic \(M\)-coefficients, \(\lim \tfrac{\log|H_1(\Gamma_N, M)_{\mathrm{tors}}|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_N]} = c_{G,M}\operatorname{vol}\), with \(t^{(2)}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(\mathrm{triv}) = -\tfrac{1}{6\pi}\) (trivial coefficients: their Conjecture 1.3, not theorem). Bianchi (non-cocompact) case: Pfaff (Ann. Glob. Anal. Geom. 2014) for strongly acyclic ranges; Raimbault (Ann. Fac. Sci. Toulouse 28 (2019)); computations Şengün (Exp. Math. 20 (2011)); the torsion Jacquet–Langlands correspondence of Calegari–Venkatesh (Astérisque 409, 2019) lives exactly on these orbifolds. Bergeron–Şengün–Venkatesh (Duke 165 (2016)): torsion growth vs. representing \(H_2\) by low-genus surfaces — note \(H_2\)-classes of Bianchi orbifolds are exactly where the Schmidt geodesic surfaces live.
- **Isospectrality.** Vignéras (Ann. of Math. 112 (1980)): quaternionic isospectral non-isometric hyperbolic 2/3-manifolds. Reid (Duke 65 (1992)): isospectral *arithmetic* hyperbolic 2/3-manifolds are commensurable. Chinburg–Hamilton–Long–Reid (Duke 145 (2008)): the rational length *set* determines the commensurability class among arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Prasad–Rapinchuk (Publ. IHES 109 (2009)): weak commensurability framework. Bartel–Page (arXiv:2407.07240): representation-theoretic Vignéras orbifolds, \(i\)-isospectral for all \(i\) yet not representation-equivalent; ties regulator/torsion differences to isospectrality — a live 2024–26 topic.
- For the project: by CHLR, the *length spectrum* of \(M\) knows the commensurability class; the analogous question for the **plane-distance spectrum** \(\{\operatorname{arccosh} n\ \text{with multiplicity}\ H(n^2-1)\}\) is untouched (§14, outlook §8).

## 11. The thin relatives: Apollonian spectral theory inside the Picard commensurability class

The Apollonian gasket group \(\mathcal{A}\) — the symmetry group of the packing formed by the atoms of the monoid \(\Omega\) of [half-plane-monoid.md](half-plane-monoid.md) — is a geometrically finite, infinite-covolume Kleinian group, *thin* in \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\)-land: its orbifold is commensurable with the Whitehead-link complement cut along a totally geodesic triply-punctured sphere (observation credited to McMullen; verified in Oh's survey, Jpn. J. Math. 9 (2014)). Its spectral theory is the infinite-volume Lax–Phillips/Patterson–Sullivan theory:

- base eigenvalue \(\lambda_0 = \delta(2-\delta)\) with \(\delta = \dim_H(\text{gasket})\) (Sullivan); \(\delta = 1.30568\,67280\ldots\), now rigorously to 128 decimals (Vytnova–Wormell, Invent. Math. 2025, arXiv:2406.04922); finitely many eigenvalues below 1, continuous spectrum \([1,\infty)\), resonances beyond.
- **Counting circles by curvature in a packing**: \(N_{\mathcal P}(T) \sim c_{\mathcal P} T^\delta\) (Kontorovich–Oh, JAMS 24 (2011), via expanding horospheres + the \(\lambda_0\)-eigenfunction); effective error \(O(T^{\delta - \eta})\) (Lee–Oh, GAFA 23 (2013)); best current effective rates: Kontorovich–Lutsko (J. Assoc. Math. Res. 2 (2024)).
- Curvature arithmetic: Bourgain–Fuchs (positive density), Bourgain–Kontorovich (density one, Invent. 196 (2014)), and the **negative resolution of local–global**: Haag–Kertzer–Rickards–Stange (Ann. of Math. 200 (2024) 749–770) — quadratic/quartic *reciprocity obstructions*, properties of the thin group itself, not of its Zariski closure. Follow-ups: Rickards–Stange (Duke 174 (2025)) — reciprocity obstructions in *semigroup* orbits in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}\)) (directly relevant to the \(\Omega\)-monoid questions of [half-plane-monoid.md](half-plane-monoid.md) §11); the Eisenstein-integer case with a modified ("Eisenpint") Schmidt arrangement: Rickards–Stange, arXiv:2605.16053 (2026).

The lattice \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) (\(\delta = 2\), \(\lambda_0 = 0\)) and the thin \(\mathcal{A}\) (\(\delta \approx 1.3057\), \(\lambda_0 \approx 0.9065\)) are the two spectral regimes that meet inside the Schmidt arrangement: the full arrangement is a lattice orbit, each tangency component is (up to the two-packings-per-circle structure of Stange's theorem) a thin orbit.

## 12. Dynamics, continued fractions, and the missing transfer operator

- **A. L. Schmidt's complex continued fractions** (Acta Math. 134 (1975) 1–85; ergodic theory: Monatsh. Math. 93 (1982) 39–62) are a subdivision system on \(\hat{\mathbb{C}}\) built from Farey triangles/circles — the same combinatorial object as the arrangement's subdivision structure. **Nakada (Monatsh. Math. 105 (1988) 131–150) realized the natural extension of Schmidt's system as a cross-section of the geodesic flow on the Picard orbifold** with the invariant measure induced from hyperbolic measure. The Hurwitz (nearest-integer) complex CF has its own ergodic theory (Hensley 2006; Ei–Ito–Nakada–Natsui, Monatsh. Math. 188 (2019); Hiary–Vandehey arXiv:1805.10151; finiteness conditions: Kalle–Sélley–Thuswaldner arXiv:2406.18689). New in 2026: Nakada–Natsui–Thuswaldner (arXiv:2603.28468) build "Farey graphs and geodesic expansions" in \(\mathbb{H}^3\) for all five Euclidean imaginary quadratic fields. Application of CF symbolic dynamics + expanders: McKeon (arXiv:1907.03350), low-lying *fundamental* geodesics in the Picard orbifold à la Bourgain–Kontorovich.
- **Mayer-type theorem: MISSING.** For the modular surface, \(Z_{\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})}(s) = \det(1-\mathcal{L}_s)\det(1+\mathcal{L}_s)\) for the Gauss-map transfer operator (Mayer, Bull. AMS 25 (1991); Lewis–Zagier period functions **[std]**; the strict-transfer-operator program of Pohl — latest Pohl–Wabnitz, Memoirs AMS 1616 (2026) — is still 2-dimensional). **No analogue exists for any Bianchi group**: no expression of the Picard Selberg zeta function as a Fredholm determinant of a complex-continued-fraction transfer operator (confirmed absent; §14). Nakada's cross-section is the natural starting point; the unique-factorization monoid \(\Omega\) of [half-plane-monoid.md](half-plane-monoid.md) is a second, arrangement-native candidate coding.
- **The Ruelle-zeta/torsion route that does work in 3D:** Fried (Invent. Math. 84 (1986)): on closed odd-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds the Ruelle zeta at 0 computes analytic torsion; Fried's conjecture in dimension 3: Dang–Guillarmou–Rivière–Shen (Invent. Math. 220 (2020)); twisted versions: Frahm–Spilioti (J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 2024). Combined with §10 this links closed-geodesic data of Picard-type manifolds to torsion homology — a working substitute for the missing Mayer theorem.

## 13. Counting relative to circles and planes: what the current technology gives

Three methods, in decreasing explicitness about the *spectrum* and increasing explicitness about *our* constants:

1. **Homogeneous dynamics (no error terms).** Oh–Shah (Invent. Math. 187 (2012), Thm 1.4): for a circle packing invariant under a *lattice* \(\Gamma < \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})\) (our case: \(\delta = 2\), Bowen–Margulis = Liouville, Patterson–Sullivan = Lebesgue), the number of circles meeting a bounded set \(E\) with curvature \(< T\) satisfies \(N_T(\mathcal{P}, E) \sim c\cdot\operatorname{Leb}(E)\,T^2\), with equidistribution of the circles' locations. Applied to \(\mathcal{S} = \Gamma\cdot\hat{\mathbb{R}}\) this is the soft version of [euclidean-counting.md](euclidean-counting.md) — the exact multiplicative formula \(N_e(n)\) and the \(X^2/2G\) constant are strictly sharper in the translation-averaged regime, and **no error term is known for regions** (Eskin–McMullen/Duke–Rudnick–Sarnak mixing methods; effective only in the thin case, §11).
2. **Common perpendiculars with explicit constants.** Parkkonen–Paulin (Bull. LMS 43 (2011) 1048–1058, read directly): the bijection \(f \mapsto \mathcal{C}_\infty(f)\) between indefinite binary Hermitian forms up to scalar and circles (anti-equivariant — the same dictionary as [circle-classification.md](circle-classification.md)); counting *representations* \(\psi_f(s) \sim \frac{\pi\,\mathrm{Covol}(\mathrm{SU}_f(\mathcal{O}_K))}{2|D_K|\zeta_K(2)\Delta(f)}\,s^2\), Gaussian case fully explicit: \(\psi_f(s) \sim \frac{\pi^2}{8\iota(f)\zeta_K(2)}\prod_{p | \Delta(f)}(1 + (\tfrac{-1}{p})p^{-1})\,s^2\). Proof via counting common perpendiculars from a horoball to the plane over \(\mathcal{C}(f)\) (their ETDS 37 (2017) machinery; error terms under exponential mixing in the Broise-Alamichel–Parkkonen–Paulin monograph, Progr. Math. 329 (2019)). They also define "\(G\)-reciprocal forms" (weight 2 when some \(g\) maps \(f\) to \(-f\)) — the published object closest to the involution \(\sigma\). This is a Schmidt-arrangement circle count in all but name — **horoball-to-plane**; the plane-to-plane (double coset) count is not there.
3. **Spectral lattice-point methods.** Point-to-point in \(\mathbb{H}^3\): classical, error records tied to the PGT technology (§6). Point-to-*plane*: Herrmann (Math. Z. 79 (1962), main term in all dimensions); Laaksonen (arXiv:1511.00580): pointwise \(O(X^{3/2})\) and \(O(X^{1+\varepsilon})\) on average — **cocompact groups only**; open for the Picard group. Plane-to-plane (= double cosets \(H\backslash\Gamma/H\) = circles by inversive distance): exists only in dimension 2, as "hyperbolic lattice-point counting in conjugacy classes": Huber; Good (\(O(X^{2/3})\)); Chatzakos–Petridis (Forum Math. 28 (2016)); the relative-trace-formula treatments Martin–McKee–Wambach (Int. J. Number Theory 7 (2011): period spectrum \(\leftrightarrow\) *ortholength spectrum* of a closed geodesic on a compact surface) and Lekkas–Petridis (arXiv:2509.12902, 2025: double cosets of two hyperbolic subgroups, \(O(X^{1/2}\log X)\) on average); \(\Omega\)-results: Voskou (arXiv:2411.12567). Also relevant: EGM, *Arithmetic applications of the hyperbolic lattice point theorem* (Proc. LMS 57 (1988) 239–283): representation numbers of binary Hermitian forms with spectral input, and their *Zeta functions of binary Hermitian forms and special values of Eisenstein series* (Math. Ann. 277 (1987) 655–708) — Fourier-coefficient identities recently completed by Flórez–Karabulut–Vu (Proc. AMS, arXiv:2002.09819): the generating series of these Hermitian zeta values *are* Eisenstein series (the \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)-analogue of Cohen–Eisenstein).

**Reciprocal geodesics** (the \(\sigma\)-relevant counting): Sarnak (Clay Math. Proc. 7 (2007) 217–237, read directly) counts, on the modular surface, geodesics conjugate to their reverses — classes \(\leftrightarrow\) dihedral subgroups, sizes \(\nu(d)\) = number of genera, asymptotics \(\Pi_{\langle\phi_R\rangle}(x) \sim \tfrac38 x\) etc. Extensions: Erlandsson–Souto (IMRN 2024, all lattices in \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})\), equidistribution), Bourgain–Kontorovich (Duke 168 (2019), low-lying reciprocal), Parkkonen–Paulin (J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 25 (2026), strongly reversible geodesics in negative curvature). **Nothing exists in hyperbolic 3-manifolds** — while our \(\sigma\)-twisted classes (geodesics orthogonal to the modular surface, trace \(-2\alpha\)) are precisely the 3-dimensional avatar.

## 14. Verified gaps

Each of the following was searched for specifically (arXiv metadata, citation graphs of Stange's papers — 3 papers total ever mention "Schmidt arrangement", none spectral — full-text probes) and **not found**:

1. **The level stratification.** No paper stratifies a Schmidt arrangement by the inversive product \(\alpha\) with \(\hat{\mathbb{R}}\); the bijection "level-\(n\) circles \(\leftrightarrow\) positive definite forms of discriminant \(1-n^2\)" and the count \(3H(n^2-1)\) appear nowhere; \(H(n^2-1)\) is not the answer to any indexed counting problem.
2. **Plane-pair spectral counting for \((\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i]), \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))\)** — the relative trace formula on \(H\backslash G/H\) with error exponents (the 3D lift of Good/Chatzakos–Petridis/Lekkas–Petridis). Even the point-to-plane problem is open for the (non-cocompact) Picard group.
3. **The period of the Picard Eisenstein series over the modular-surface cycle** with its class-number expansion; more broadly, any closed-form Bianchi-form period over \(Y\). (Frameworks ready: Jacquet–Lai residue, Lapid–Rogawski, Zagier-type regularization.)
4. **A Mayer-type theorem for any Bianchi group** (Selberg zeta as Fredholm determinant of a complex-CF transfer operator). Nakada's 1988 cross-section and the \(\Omega\)-monoid coding are the available starting points.
5. **Reciprocal-geodesic theory in 3D**, in particular for the \(\sigma\)-twisted classes with trace \(-2\alpha\); no Cartan-embedding involution on circle classes appears in the literature (\([f] \mapsto [\mathfrak{r}_n][f]^{-1}\) is new).
6. **An Eichler–Selberg trace formula over \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)** organized by binary Hermitian class numbers (the weight-3/2-style generating objects exist: EGM 1987, Flórez–Karabulut–Vu).
7. **A relative Hecke-type structure on \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[i])/\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\)** with arithmetic spherical-function expansions (the archimedean de Sitter analysis — Faraut, Molchanov, Strichartz — has no arithmetic quotient counterpart).

These seven gaps, together with the dictionary of §1 and the anomaly of §7, are the input to [spectral-outlook.md](spectral-outlook.md).
